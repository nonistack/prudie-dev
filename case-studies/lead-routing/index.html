Lead routing, from form to CRM to Slack

A small business was losing leads in the gap between a form submission and a rep actually seeing it. This is the workflow that closed that gap: what it does, how it is built, and what happens when a step fails.

The problem: leads sat in an inbox

What was actually happening before the build.

A form on the client's site sent every new lead to a shared inbox. Someone had to open it, read the details, decide who covered that territory or industry, copy the information into the CRM by hand, and ping the rep in Slack. When that person was busy, out sick, or simply behind, leads sat for hours before anyone touched them. By the time a rep reached out, some buyers had already booked a call with a competitor.

Nothing in that chain told anyone it was slow. The only signal was a rep noticing, days or weeks later, that a lead had gone cold.

What I built: a pipeline that runs itself

The system that replaced the inbox.

A self-hosted n8n workflow now picks up every submission the moment it arrives. It fills in the missing details, applies the client's routing rules to decide who owns the lead, writes the record to the CRM, and notifies that rep directly. No one has to watch an inbox for the handoff to happen. The rep's first message about a new lead is the notification telling them it is already assigned and already in the CRM.

How it works: four stages, one path for every lead

The pipeline every submission moves through.

Every lead, regardless of source, goes through the same four stages in order. A stage either completes or it fails and hands off to the recovery path covered below.

Intake

The form or webhook fires and the raw submission is written to the queue immediately, before any processing happens.

Enrich

Missing fields get filled in: company size, domain, and source, pulled from the data already available to the workflow.

Decide

The client's routing rules match the enriched lead to an owner by territory, industry, or current rotation.

Write

The lead is logged to the CRM and the owning rep is notified in the channel they actually check.

How failures are handled: retried, then escalated, never dropped

What happens when a stage fails.

A dropped lead costs more than a slow one, so every stage assumes the next one can fail. The workflow was designed around the assumption that something eventually will.

A stage fails once
The failed step is retried automatically on a short delay before anyone is told. Most transient failures, like a CRM API timing out, resolve on the retry and the lead moves on without anyone noticing.
A stage keeps failing
After a set number of retries, the workflow stops retrying and raises an alert in a channel the client chose, with the full lead record attached so a person can finish the handoff by hand.
Every failure leaves a record
Every failed run is logged centrally, so a missed alert still leaves a trail someone can pick up later.
